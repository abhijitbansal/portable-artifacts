# Type of film: explainer

One idea, taught through one worked example. For a `pro` film, usually 16:9 and about 30 seconds.

## When

The user wants a viewer to understand a thing: how a tool works, why a decision was made, what a result means. It fits a product walk-through, a recap and a concept. If the film is mostly numbers use the data page; if it is a short for a feed use the vertical page.

## Shape

- Problem, the idea, proof, ask. Open with the problem in the viewer's words, not the product's. The proof is the worked example, shown while it is explained.
- Follow one example from the first shot to the last. A second example means a second film.
- Pace is 150 words a minute: a shot of 3 to 6 seconds holds about 8 to 15 words, and a picture may sit still for 6 seconds.
- End on one ask, in an `end` shot.
- The engine draws cuts. It has no continuous take and no endless zoom, so give the film its motion with cuts that mean something.

## Devices

- A worked example drawn in place: a `diagram` that grows node by node as each is named.
- Before and after: a `compare` set to its before and after mode.
- A `number` that lands on the word that says it.
- A `match` cut that carries one object from a shot to the next.
- Cuts on the beat, with `music.snap`, when the film has a music bed, and a `circle` on the word that names the thing to see.

## Check

- The one thing fits in a sentence, every shot serves it, and the teaching check in `references/story.md` is filled in.
- The same example runs from the first shot to the last.
- The picture shows the idea in at least one shot, not only the narration.
- The last shot is one ask, not a recap.

# Type of film: data

One claim per chart. For a `pro` film about numbers, with `ledger` as its natural look.

## When

The user has a table, a metric or a result and wants it understood, not only shown: a quarter, a survey, a benchmark. If the numbers serve a story about how something works, use the explainer page.

## Shape

- Claim, evidence, so what. Say the claim in the first sentence of the shot, then let the chart prove it.
- One claim per chart. A second claim is a second shot, even on the same data.
- The highlighted mark is what the claim is about, and it turns on the word that names it. Say what the viewer should see, not only what the bars are.
- Name the unit and the source on screen; every figure in a `say` is in the data the user gave.
- Pace is 150 words a minute, and a picture may sit still for 6 seconds, so a chart can be read before the next line.

## Devices

- A `chart` with its `highlight` set on the mark the claim is about.
- A `number` that lands for the one figure the film is built around.
- A `compare` for a before and after, or two options.
- A `timeline` when the data is a sequence of dated events.
- A `map` when the claim is about where, and a `circle` on the word that names the mark.

## Check

- Each chart has a `highlight` (leave it out only when the claim is the pattern as a whole), a title that states the claim and a `source`.
- No chart has more marks than the claim needs; the kind itself caps them.
- Every number spoken is in the user's data, and is written as it is said.
- The film ends on what to do with the finding, in one ask.
